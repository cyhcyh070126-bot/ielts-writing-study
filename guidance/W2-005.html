展开审题、观点与写法

不同角度怎样展开

这些是备选论证方向，不是唯一正确立场。选择后要回应题目范围。

  • 优势：支付结算较快、记录清晰，可减少携带现金和对账成本。
  • 劣势：没有设备或账户的人被排除；断电、故障时无法买生活必需品。
  • 权衡标准：节约的少量交易时间与基本服务可达性比较；数字支付普及与彻底取消现金要分开。

本题审题与一种计划

  • 两边都写：便利和记账收益，以及排斥、故障等代价。
  • 评价的是只接受数字支付的制度，不是单纯提供数字支付选项。
  • 明确比较标准，例如基本购买的可及性与系统韧性，并给出哪方更重的判断。

在只接受数字支付的前提下，弊大于利；便利收益不足以抵消基本购买被排除与故障时无法交易的风险。

  • 引言：承认便利，但明确对纯无现金制度持弊大于利判断。
  • 主体一：认真解释快捷支付、查账的好处。
  • 主体二：说明可及性和故障风险，并按基本需求与便利程度进行权衡。
  • 结论：重申比较结果，建议保留现金或等效的可及备选。

论证链

  • 数字支付便捷并自动留记录 → 排队和记账负担下降 → 对消费者与商家均有实际收益。
  • 只接受数字支付 → 部分人或故障期间无法购买必需品 → 基本可及性比少排队更重要 → 因而弊更重。

易错点

  • 只写无现金支付很方便，完全不写其弊端。
  • 把only digital payments写成cash and digital都可以，改变比较对象。
  • 把三条优点对两条缺点当作利大于弊的证明。
句框 · 按内容填槽

引言

[Policy or development] offers clear benefits in terms of [benefit]. Nevertheless, I believe that its [advantages/disadvantages] are greater, particularly when judged against [comparison standard].

先确定到底谁大于谁；比较对象必须与题目中的制度范围一致。

主体一

The main benefits of [development] are [benefit one] and [benefit two]. [Explain the mechanism]. For example, [illustration]. This can [practical improvement]. These gains are significant for [affected people or situation].

即使这一方最终较弱，也要认真展开，不能用一句话敷衍。

主体二

However, [the other side] carries greater weight because [explicit comparison]. [Explain the more serious effect]. For example, [specific case]. Unlike [less serious or more reversible effect], this can [harder-to-remedy consequence]. Therefore, [state the comparative judgement].

比较严重程度、影响范围、可逆性或必要性，不是数优点缺点各有几条。

结论

In conclusion, although [acknowledge weaker side], I believe that [stronger side] outweighs it because [decisive standard]. [Optional brief implication already supported in the body].

用because说明判断依据；不要只写利大于弊然后原样重复。

完整原创范文

Digital payments can make purchases quick and easy to track, but a system that excludes cash creates a separate set of problems. I believe that the disadvantages of this exclusive arrangement outweigh its advantages, especially for essential everyday purchases.

The main advantages are convenience and clearer records. A customer can pay for a bus ticket or a meal without finding the correct coins, while the payment appears automatically in a statement that can help with budgeting. For businesses, quicker transactions may shorten queues at busy times and reduce the work involved in counting and storing cash. A small cafe, for example, can serve a lunchtime crowd more smoothly when most customers pay electronically. These improvements are useful, and there is a strong case for making digital payment widely available alongside other options.

However, requiring everyone to use it can prevent some people from buying goods at all. Older customers, people without suitable bank accounts and those whose phones are lost or broken may struggle to complete even a basic purchase. A network failure can also stop an entire shop from taking payments, leaving customers unable to buy food despite having sufficient money. These costs carry more weight than a modest reduction in queueing time because access to necessities is more important than making an already possible transaction slightly faster. Moreover, retaining cash as a backup preserves much of the convenience of digital payment while avoiding unnecessary exclusion and improving resilience during technical failures.

In conclusion, digital payments should be encouraged, but the convenience of a fully cashless system does not outweigh its risks to inclusion and reliable access to essential goods.
