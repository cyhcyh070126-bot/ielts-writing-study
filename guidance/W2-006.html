展开审题、观点与写法

不同角度怎样展开

这些是备选论证方向，不是唯一正确立场。选择后要回应题目范围。

  • 问题：劳动者只能住得远或频繁搬家→通勤、休息和工作稳定性受影响。
  • 措施：在就业地附近增加适合普通收入者的住房，并改善公共交通；明确由谁落实。
  • 匹配：补贴、供给、交通分别解决哪一个压力？不可只建价格更高的住宅却不解释可负担性。

本题审题与一种计划

  • 主体一写住房负担造成的后果，不把整段写成房价为什么上涨。
  • 主体二提出能缓解这些后果的措施。
  • 范围是城市年轻劳动者，例子与措施应与其收入和通勤有关。

重点分析拥挤或远距离居住造成的生活压力及就业限制，再用长期可负担租赁与交通、供给安排回应。

  • 引言：概括居住与就业问题，点出措施方向。
  • 主体一：解释挤住或远住如何损害健康、时间与职业选择。
  • 主体二：提出稳定可负担租赁、就业地区附近供给及交通安排。
  • 结论：汇总问题和与之对应的措施。

论证链

  • 住房负担重 → 挤住或搬到远处 → 休息与通勤受损 → 生活质量下降并限制岗位选择。
  • 在就业和公交附近增加稳定可负担租赁 → 降低居住压力和搬家不确定性 → 配套交通扩大可选区域 → 改善就业可及性。

易错点

  • 问题段全部写人口流入、土地稀缺等成因，遗漏题目问的后果。
  • 写政府应重视、社会要合作，却没有实际行动。
  • 措施只说建更多豪宅，与可负担住房困难无对应关系。
句框 · 按内容填槽

引言

Difficulty in [specific situation] can create serious problems for [group]. The main consequences concern [problem areas], and [responsible actors] can respond through [broad measures].

回答问题与措施，不必硬加同意程度。

主体一

One consequence is [problem]. When [situation], [explain how the harm occurs]. For example, [concrete illustration]. A further problem is [second consequence]. This can [further impact on the group or society].

把原因与结果区分清楚：这段写已经发生的困难会造成什么。

主体二

To address [problem one], [actor] should [specific action]. This would [explain how the action reduces the harm]. For example, [implementation detail]. [Actor] could also [measure targeting problem two]. [Explain the intended effect and a necessary condition].

措施要有执行者、动作和作用机制，与前文问题逐项对应。

结论

In conclusion, [situation] can lead to [main harms]. [Measure one] and [measure two] would help by [brief explanation of the shared purpose].

避免在结尾突然补充第三个重大问题或措施。

完整原创范文

Unaffordable housing can make living and working in a large city unnecessarily difficult for younger employees. Its main consequences are insecure living conditions and restricted career choices, which require measures that improve both affordable accommodation and access to employment.

One major problem is that workers may accept crowded or unsuitable homes simply to remain near their jobs. Sharing a small flat with several people can make sleep, privacy and quiet study difficult, particularly for those working irregular hours. Others move far from the centre and lose a substantial part of each day to travel, leaving little time for exercise or family responsibilities. Housing costs can also prevent a worker from accepting a useful entry-level position if the salary cannot cover local rent. Over time, cities may consequently lose potential nurses, technicians or other essential staff despite having vacancies that those workers could fill.

A practical response is to expand stable rental housing near employment and public transport. Local authorities could make suitable public land available for homes with clear affordability conditions, rather than allowing every development to target the highest-paying tenants. For example, an agreement could reserve part of a new development for workers on moderate incomes and offer longer leases that make relocation less disruptive. Better transport connections would also allow people to live in less expensive districts without sacrificing hours to unreliable journeys. These measures should be coordinated, since a cheap flat offers limited help if reaching work remains slow, costly or impossible during early and late shifts.

In conclusion, excessive housing costs can damage young workers' daily lives and employment prospects, while affordable rentals linked to dependable transport would reduce both forms of pressure.
