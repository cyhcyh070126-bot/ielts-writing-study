展开审题、观点与写法

不同角度怎样展开

这些是备选论证方向，不是唯一正确立场。选择后要回应题目范围。

  • 限流：当游客超过道路、供水或文化遗址容量时，限制高峰人数能减少拥堵和磨损。
  • 继续接待：旅游收入支持当地餐馆、导游和住宿业，全面限流可能减少岗位。
  • 权衡：按地点容量预约、错峰入场；比较居民生活与就业，而不只说旅游好或坏。

本题审题与一种计划

  • 解释限制游客的理由。
  • 解释继续欢迎大量游客的理由。
  • 明确个人判断，并说明判断依据。

认可旅游带来的就业与收入，但在承载能力有限的热门地点支持有针对性的预约限流。

  • 引言：概括双方，明确支持针对性限流。
  • 主体一：解释欢迎游客对当地企业和就业的价值。
  • 主体二：解释过量游客对居民与遗产的损害，并说明为何更支持限制。
  • 结论：概括双方价值，重申承载能力优先的判断。

论证链

  • 游客消费 → 小旅馆、餐饮等获得收入 → 家庭就业和公共服务得到支持 → 说明继续欢迎游客的合理性。
  • 超过承载能力 → 环境受损、居民生活受挤压 → 损害难以逆转 → 限流并分散季节与地点更有长期价值。

易错点

  • 只论证自己支持限流，没有解释欢迎更多游客这一方。
  • 把讨论双方写成一段好处、一段措施，遗漏一方观点。
  • 结论才突然出现一个主体段从未支持的立场。
句框 · 按内容填槽

引言

People disagree about whether [view A] or [view B]. Although [brief recognition of one side], I believe that [your judgement].

讨论题在引言里就交代个人判断，不必拖到最后才表态。

主体一

Those who favour [view A] argue that [reason]. [Explain why that reason is persuasive]. For example, [illustration]. [Explain the resulting benefit or concern]. This helps to explain why [group] supports this approach.

认真解释对方观点的合理性，避免只写一句有人这么想。

主体二

Supporters of [view B], however, emphasise [different concern]. [Explain its mechanism]. For instance, [illustration]. I find this argument stronger because [comparison or judgement]. [Explain how the preferred approach could work].

个人意见可以嵌入讨论，但要给出为什么更赞成这一方的理由。

结论

In conclusion, while [fair summary of one view], I believe that [preferred position], particularly when [relevant condition].

有条件的判断仍是明确立场；条件应能从主体段找到依据。

完整原创范文

Popular destinations must balance the income generated by tourism with the pressure visitors place on local life. Although continued growth can support businesses, I believe that particularly crowded sites should limit visitor numbers when their capacity is being exceeded.

People who oppose limits usually emphasise the livelihoods that depend on tourist spending. Hotels, restaurants and small shops need customers, and a strict cap could reduce income for families who have few alternative sources of employment. For instance, a family running a small guesthouse may depend on the busy season to cover repairs and living costs throughout the year. More visitors can also support bus services or cultural activities that local residents use. From this perspective, restricting arrivals may seem to protect a place by removing the economic activity that helps it function.

However, supporters of limits argue that unrestricted growth can damage the very qualities that attract visitors. Narrow historic streets can become difficult for residents to use, while fragile paths may deteriorate faster than they can be repaired. A coastal village, for example, gains little from additional day trips if its waste system cannot cope and its public spaces become unusable. I find this argument stronger at vulnerable sites because physical damage and the loss of residents are difficult to reverse. Advance booking, timed entry and limits at the busiest locations can protect these places while directing visitors towards quieter seasons or nearby attractions where businesses can still benefit. This approach protects the attractions on which future tourism income depends.

In conclusion, tourism income deserves protection, but visitor growth should remain within a destination's environmental and social capacity, with targeted limits used where continued crowding threatens its long-term future.
