展开审题、观点与写法

不同角度怎样展开

这些是备选论证方向，不是唯一正确立场。选择后要回应题目范围。

  • 原因：可安全活动的空间少、接送和照看时间受限，使儿童即使想运动也难参加。
  • 措施：学校开放场地、社区组织低成本活动、家长安排可持续的日常户外时段。
  • 条件：安全、费用、接送与年龄共同影响参与；把屏幕娱乐当唯一原因会过度概括。

本题审题与一种计划

  • 原因指解释为什么活动不足，而非只列肥胖等后果。
  • 措施同时涉及家长与当地社区。
  • 范围为校外活动，不能只建议学校增加体育课。

将缺乏安全可及空间、家长时间与安排困难作为原因，再分别给出社区和家长能执行的回应。

  • 引言：概括校外活动不足的原因与可回应主体。
  • 主体一：解释活动空间和家庭安排的限制机制。
  • 主体二：社区改善空间和活动组织，家长提供可持续的日常安排。
  • 结论：概括与原因相配套的措施。

论证链

  • 忙碌家庭加上危险道路、收费场地 → 运动需要额外时间和成本 → 室内娱乐更容易发生 → 校外活动不足。
  • 社区提供近便安全空间＋家长把活动嵌入原有行程 → 组织成本下降 → 儿童更容易长期坚持。

易错点

  • 把肥胖、注意力下降等后果写成儿童不运动的原因。
  • 只让学校多开体育课，忽略outside school。
  • 家长、社区二选一，漏掉题目明确要求的另一个主体。
句框 · 按内容填槽

引言

There are several reasons why [group] may [behaviour or situation]. The most important concern [cause one] and [cause two], and both [actor one] and [actor two] can help address them.

先交代原因，不把问题后果当原因；注意题目点名的所有执行者。

主体一

One reason is [cause]. [Explain the route from this cause to the behaviour]. For example, [specific situation]. A second factor is [another cause]. This makes [behaviour] more likely because [mechanism].

原因段回答为什么会这样，写出中间步骤，不停留在名词列表。

主体二

[Actor one] could respond by [action targeting cause one]. This would [effect]. Meanwhile, [actor two] should [action targeting cause two]. For example, [manageable routine or implementation]. Together, these changes would [desired behaviour].

明确谁做什么，家长与社区各有职责，措施不全部丢给学校。

结论

In conclusion, [cause one] and [cause two] help explain [situation]. Reducing these barriers through [paired measures] would make [desired behaviour] easier to sustain.

措施应能持续执行，不只写举行一次宣传活动。

完整原创范文

Children may spend little time being active outside school even when they enjoy games and sport. Two important reasons are the lack of accessible places to play and difficulties in organising family time, both of which parents and communities can address.

The first barrier is that outdoor activity is not always an easy or safe option. A child living beside busy roads may be unable to reach a park independently, while a nearby sports centre may charge more than the family can regularly afford. In such circumstances, staying indoors is often the simplest choice rather than evidence that the child dislikes exercise. Family schedules create another obstacle, particularly when parents return home late and must organise dinner, homework and preparation for the following day. Booking a distant class then feels like an additional task, whereas watching a video requires almost no planning or transport.

Local communities can help by providing safe, inexpensive opportunities close to where families live. Opening a community hall for supervised games or improving crossings on the route to a park would remove practical barriers rather than merely telling children to exercise. Parents, meanwhile, can build manageable activity into routines they already follow, such as walking part of the journey home or playing outside before dinner. They should also offer a choice of activities, since a child who dislikes competitive football may enjoy dancing, cycling or informal games. When adults join in and keep these routines realistic, physical activity becomes a normal part of family life rather than another compulsory appointment.

In conclusion, limited local opportunities and busy household routines can discourage activity, while accessible community spaces and simple family habits would make regular movement easier for children.
