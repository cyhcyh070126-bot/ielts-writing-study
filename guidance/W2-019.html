  • 家庭价值：可靠、守诺和自律帮助一个人持续利用机会，并影响与他人合作。
  • 学校技能：特定知识和任务能力可能决定是否取得某些学习或职业机会。
  • 比较：用同一种成功标准或不同阶段解释相对重要性，不把values和skills混成一回事。

    句框 · 按内容填槽

    可用句框

    [A] can strongly influence [one stage of success], particularly when [condition]. Yet [B] is often more decisive at [a different stage], because [specific barrier].

    可用句框

    Consider two people who share [quality A]. Only the one who has acquired [skill B] may be able to [perform a relevant task].

    完整原创范文

    The values children learn from their families can have a lasting effect on how they approach work and relationships. I agree that these values matter greatly, but I do not consider them consistently more influential than what children learn in school. Their relative importance changes with the demands a person faces.

    A family can give children habits that make it easier to take advantage of later opportunities. A child who is expected to keep promises and finish agreed tasks has repeated opportunities to learn responsibility. At work, these habits may show up in such ordinary but important actions as telling colleagues early when a deadline will be missed. This makes it easier for other people to plan around a problem and can build the trust needed to take on more demanding work. In that sense, values can affect how far someone progresses even after formal education has ended.

    However, school knowledge can determine whether someone has access to an opportunity at all. Two equally reliable applicants for a laboratory placement might both be willing to work hard, but the one who can calculate concentrations and read a chart is better prepared to carry out the required tasks. School also exposes children to work they are unlikely to encounter at home. A pupil who completes a practical science project may discover an ability that neither the pupil nor the family had recognised. That discovery, together with the knowledge gained through the project, can influence the decision to pursue further training.

    Overall, family values help people use opportunities responsibly, while skills acquired in school can make those opportunities available. I therefore see both as important, with the balance depending on the kind and stage of success being considered.
