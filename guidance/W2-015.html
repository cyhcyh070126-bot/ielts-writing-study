展开审题、观点与写法

不同角度怎样展开

这些是备选论证方向，不是唯一正确立场。选择后要回应题目范围。

  • 年龄角度：人生阶段会带来不同需要，例如独立居住时集中购置基本用品。
  • 其他因素：预算和渠道决定能买什么、在哪里买，同龄人也可能有完全不同的购买限制。
  • 比较标准：同年龄不同收入、不同年龄相似预算；回应首要因素，而非平行列三个影响。

本题审题与一种计划

    大体不同意。年龄和人生阶段可以改变购物偏好，但预算与购物渠道会更直接地决定买什么、在哪里买、买得多频繁。

    • 承认年龄影响 → 不同人生阶段形成不同需求或熟悉不同购买渠道 → 刚离开父母独自居住的人可能集中购置用品，已有成套用品的人更常按需替换。
    • 检验哪个因素更强 → 用同年龄不同预算与不同年龄同预算两种比较 → 钱够不够买、附近能不能买，都会直接约束行为 → 比年龄本身更能解释许多选择。
    • 避免刻板印象 → 同龄人可以有不同习惯。用一种商品或同一个购买情境比较，别假定所有年轻人网购、所有老年人只去实体店。

    易错点

    • 题目用 more than any other factor 的变体时，至少比较一个其他因素的作用大小。主体段逐个列年龄、收入、广告却不作权重比较，无法直接支持同意或不同意。
    完整原创范文

    Age can help explain what people buy and how often they shop. However, I largely disagree that it is the strongest influence on shopping habits. Income and access to shops often have a more direct effect because they determine which purchases are practical in the first place.

    There is some reason to associate shopping patterns with age. A person who has just left home may buy cookware, bedding and other essentials within a short period. An established household is more likely to replace these items occasionally. Familiarity with different shopping methods can matter too. Someone accustomed to comparing products on a phone may check several retailers before making a purchase, whereas someone used to buying locally might start by looking in nearby shops. Such patterns explain why age can be a useful starting point when considering consumer behaviour.

    Nevertheless, two people of the same age can develop very different shopping routines because their budgets and local options differ. Consider two thirty-year-olds buying groceries. One can afford to pay a delivery fee and order a week's food online. The other needs to limit spending and can reach a discount supermarket on foot, so may visit it regularly to buy items on offer. Equally, two people from different age groups may both rely on delivery if the nearest shop is difficult to reach. In these cases, knowing what customers can afford and where they can shop explains more than their ages alone.

    In conclusion, age can shape people's needs and preferences, but income and access often decide which of those preferences translate into regular purchases. I therefore regard these practical conditions as stronger influences in many situations.
