    可用句框

    The case for [action] is strongest when [cause] is within our control and [a practical intervention] can improve [the outcome].

    完整原创范文

    Animal species have always disappeared as environments have changed. However, I largely disagree that this gives humans no reason to act. The origins of a threat and the chances of reversing it are more relevant to conservation decisions than the fact that extinction can occur naturally.

    There are sensible limits to what conservation programmes should attempt. When a population is very small and the conditions it needs can no longer be restored, keeping a few individuals alive may require considerable spending with little prospect of a lasting recovery. The same funds might protect a site where several threatened populations could still reproduce. A conservation agency should therefore compare the likely results of different projects before investing in them. This is a case for choosing interventions carefully, even when a species is much loved.

    The argument for intervention is much stronger when the pressure is caused by people and can be reduced. Suppose that a bird population is declining because the shallow wetlands where it breeds are being drained. Protecting the remaining pools and restoring water to some nearby fields can provide places for birds to nest again. This changes the conditions that caused the decline, so the birds have a chance to recover without being kept permanently in captivity. There is also a practical benefit in protecting a habitat used by several species at once. A restored wetland may support insects and amphibians as well as the birds that initially motivated the project.

    In conclusion, a natural process can still be influenced by human decisions. Preventing extinction is worth attempting when people can remove a major threat and have a credible plan for the survival of the species in the wild.
