展开审题、观点与写法

不同角度怎样展开

这些是备选论证方向，不是唯一正确立场。选择后要回应题目范围。

  • 为何争办：短期游客支出、城市知名度和配套设施可成为地方政府争取赛事的动机。
  • 支持公共投入：已有场馆、长期公共用途清楚时，部分设施可能服务居民。
  • 反对无条件投入：超支和闲置设施挤占住房、交通等预算；评价要回到公共机会成本。

本题审题与一种计划

  • 第一问解释城市争办的动机，不直接评价花钱是否合理。
  • 第二问明确公款支出立场并说明条件。
  • 不能只写举办赛事的好处，而遗漏机会成本与当地公共需求。

城市希望提升知名度、吸引消费并推动建设；只有预算透明、设施有长期用途且不挤压基本服务时，有限公款投入才合理。

  • 引言：简要回答两问，指出吸引力与有条件支持。
  • 主体一：回答为什么想办，解释宣传、消费与城市建设动机。
  • 主体二：回答该不该花公款，以公共收益、机会成本和后续使用为标准。
  • 结论：把动机与支出判断各概括一次。

论证链

  • 国际赛事曝光与游客消费 → 城市希望提高知名度和企业收入 → 同时借期限推动建设 → 解释争办动机。
  • 公款存在其他用途且设施需长期维护 → 核验后续用途、总成本和基本服务 → 符合条件才投入 → 回答该不该花钱而非重复动机。

易错点

  • 整篇介绍赛事好处，未回答是否应该使用公款。
  • 第一问为什么想办与第二问是否值得完全写成同一段论证。
  • 有条件支持却不给条件，或只说政府应该考虑很多因素。
句框 · 按内容填槽

引言

[Situation] is often motivated by [brief answer to question one]. In my view, [direct answer to question two], provided that [key condition if relevant].

引言可以用两句直接回答两问，不使用只说本文将讨论的空壳。

主体一

There are two main reasons why [answer the first question]. First, [reason and explanation]. For example, [specific illustration]. Second, [another reason and its mechanism]. This helps explain [the behaviour or choice described in the prompt].

段落职责始终是第一问；本题需解释动机而不是已经证明支出合理。

主体二

Regarding [the second question], I believe [clear answer]. [Give the main reason or criterion]. For instance, [case showing when the judgement applies]. However, [relevant limit or alternative scenario]. Therefore, [explain the practical boundary of your position].

第二问可能是评价而非原因，必须换成相应的论证职责。

结论

In conclusion, [concise answer to question one]. [Concise answer to question two, consistent with the body].

结论可两句，不必为了十三句练习格式删掉某一问。

完整原创范文

Cities often seek major sporting events because they hope to gain international attention and stimulate local economic activity. I believe that some public investment can be justified, but only when the event serves a credible long-term plan rather than becoming an expensive display.

One important motivation is the opportunity to make a city visible to people who might otherwise know little about it. Television coverage can introduce its streets, landscape and cultural attractions to potential visitors, while spectators spend money in local hotels, shops and restaurants. A city may therefore see the event as a way to support businesses and strengthen its reputation. Hosting can also give politicians a deadline for improving transport or renovating neglected public areas. A rail connection that has been delayed for years, for example, may finally receive coordinated funding because it is needed to move large numbers of spectators.

However, these ambitions do not automatically justify using public money. Governments should first establish whether facilities will remain useful and whether essential services can be maintained without cuts. Renovating a sports centre that residents will later use is easier to defend than building a large specialised stadium with no regular users after the event. The budget should also include maintenance and provide a clear explanation of who will cover unexpected costs, rather than relying on optimistic expectations of visitor spending. If an event would leave schools or local transport underfunded, or create facilities that the city cannot afford to operate, the government should scale down its plans or decline to host.

In conclusion, publicity and development opportunities explain cities' interest in sporting events. Public funding is reasonable only when lasting community benefits and a realistic budget outweigh the alternative uses of that money.
