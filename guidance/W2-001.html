展开审题、观点与写法

不同角度怎样展开

这些是备选论证方向，不是唯一正确立场。选择后要回应题目范围。

  • 支持替代的角度：标准讲解和即时反馈可重复提供，偏远地区也能取得基础教学资源。进一步解释哪些任务能标准化。
  • 反对替代的角度：教师观察学生的情绪和理解困难，选择不同活动；还要核验AI输出并对课堂负责。
  • 折中条件：把AI用于练习和初步反馈，保留教师主导。讨论的是replace，不是能否辅助使用。

本题审题与一种计划

  • 回应的是取代教师这一主张，不是泛泛评价AI好不好。
  • 明确同意程度；本篇完全反对取代，但认可AI作为辅助工具。
  • 两个主体段分别论证教师的育人作用与对学习质量负责的作用。

完全反对让AI取代教师；辅助使用AI与反对取代并不矛盾。完全同意题也可使用两个主体段支持同一立场的结构，但理由必须重新选择。

  • 引言：改写取代主张，明确反对。
  • 主体一：解释课堂教学为什么需要观察学生、调整方法与情感支持。
  • 主体二：解释AI答案为何仍需教师筛选、核验并融入课程。
  • 结论：再次回答取代问题，给出教师主导、AI辅助的判断。

论证链

  • 教师观察到学生回避朗读 → 判断困难可能来自焦虑 → 调整练习并鼓励 → 学生获得个别支持。
  • AI回答可能有误 → 学生难以独立识别 → 教师示范核查证据 → 形成判断能力而非依赖现成答案。

易错点

  • 只讨论AI方便、便宜，没有回应能否取代教师。
  • 说完全反对后，主体段又主张大部分学校取消教师。
  • 把两段都写成教师很重要，没有不同的作用机制。
句框 · 按内容填槽

引言

[Technology or policy] is changing how [specific activity] is carried out. I completely [agree/disagree] with the proposal to [exact action in the question], because [reason one] and [reason two].

agree或disagree只能选一个；反对题目中的具体主张，避免只写笼统的科技利弊。

主体一

The first reason for my position is that [specific reason]. When [concrete situation], [explain what happens]. For example, [a realistic illustration]. This allows/prevents [direct consequence]. Such [benefit or risk] matters particularly for [affected group].

一条理由写深：交代谁在什么情境下受到什么影响。

主体二

A further consideration is [a different reason]. [Explain the mechanism]. For instance, [another concrete situation]. Without [necessary condition], [likely consequence]. This is why [connect the reasoning to the exact proposal].

第二段提供不同的理由，不把第一段换同义词再写一次。

结论

In conclusion, I [support/oppose] [the precise proposal], since [brief synthesis of the two reasons]. [Optional: clarify the limited role of an alternative].

全文用词可以变化，立场保持一致；不在结尾突然改成部分同意。

完整原创范文

AI systems can already offer explanations and give students rapid feedback, which has led some people to suggest removing human teachers from schools. I completely disagree with this proposal, although I believe that such systems can be valuable classroom tools.

Teaching involves responding to children as individuals rather than simply delivering correct information. A teacher can notice that a normally confident pupil has become withdrawn, or that several students are pretending to understand an explanation. These observations allow the teacher to change the activity, ask a simpler question or arrange a private conversation. For example, a child who repeatedly avoids reading aloud may need encouragement and carefully chosen practice rather than another automated test. Replacing the teacher would make it harder to connect academic difficulties with the emotional and social circumstances that often cause them. A patient adult can also build trust before a pupil feels ready to discuss a sensitive concern during a difficult period.

Human teachers are also needed to take responsibility for how educational technology is used. An AI answer may sound convincing while containing an error, and students may lack the knowledge required to challenge it. A teacher can compare that answer with reliable materials and turn the mistake into a lesson about checking evidence. Similarly, teachers can decide when immediate automated feedback is useful and when students should struggle independently before receiving help. If schools merely give every child an AI account, they risk encouraging dependence on ready-made explanations instead of developing the judgement that education should build.

In conclusion, AI should support teachers by handling selected routine tasks, while human educators remain responsible for pupils' learning, wellbeing and ability to think independently.
